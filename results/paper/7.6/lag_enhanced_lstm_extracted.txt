    In this research, preprocessing involved two main steps:
handling missing values and aligning data across stations,
implemented using Python with pandas[18] and NumPy[19].
Frequency data from each station was cleaned individually.
Missing values were filled with the station's own mean to
preserve its characteristics [20]. Stations with only missing
data or zero variance were excluded, and all values were
converted to a consistent numeric format.
    To analyze lagged relationships such as t – 1 or t – 2, we
shifted each station’s time series by a fixed number of rows,
where each row corresponds to a 20-millisecond interval. For
example, a lag – 3 represents a 60 ms forward shift. This         Fig. 1. Frequency Comparison Between Station A (t−1) and Station B (t)
approach allowed us to model short-term dependencies across
stations at multiple temporal offsets. All stations contributed
both as references and lagged targets, forming a fully
connected structure that captures how frequency changes
propagate across the grid.
B. Lag-Based Correlation Setup
    We computed pairwise cross-correlations across lag
intervals from t – 1 to t – 10 for all station combinations.
Several station pairs showed correlation values consistently
above 0.997 across multiple lag intervals, indicating strong
temporal relationships between frequency patterns at different
grid locations. Each station contributed to the correlation
process both as a reference point at time t and as a target at
shifted time steps.

B. Temporal and Spatial Patterns of Delay
    To understand how stations organize based on delay
behavior, hierarchical clustering was applied to the delay
matrix. Fig. 6 presents a hierarchical clustering dendrogram
based on the delay matrix, where the vertical axis shows the
linkage distance between stations. The clustering is derived
from average delays at which each station pair reached
maximum correlation, reflecting how similarly each node
responds to or influences others over time. Three clusters were
formed at a linkage threshold of 50 ms.
    Cluster 1 contains the majority of stations including A, I,
M, K, F, and others, indicating these nodes share similar
temporal behavior and respond to frequency changes within
comparable timeframes. Their grouping suggests that they are
part of a tightly connected region in the grid where
disturbances propagate with minimal delay.
