用下列单段文本替换当前 `\begin{abstract}` 与 `\end{abstract}` 之间未被注释的旧摘要：

```latex
This paper develops a slope-aware perception-to-control framework for path
tracking of a diagonal dual-drive-and-steer automated guided vehicle using
linear parameter-varying model predictive control (LPV-MPC). ModernTCN-delta
estimates longitudinal road grade from a history-only proprioceptive window
augmented with multi-scale temporal differences, while a qualified causal
force-balance observer supplies complementary information through an
uncertainty-adaptive correction calibrated from empirical total-error moments,
cross-source dependence, and online innovation consistency. The correction is
bounded and rate-limited, retains ModernTCN as the primary source, and falls
back exactly to it when the observer is invalid or the innovation is rejected.
The conditioned grade is applied consistently to the LPV prediction model,
measured-disturbance channel, slope feedforward, scheduled weights, and input
envelopes. Under a frozen MATLAB/Simulink protocol with four estimators, six
closed-loop routes, and ten model seeds, truth-driven scheduling improved all
three primary outcomes relative to zero-grade LPV-MPC, establishing the control
relevance of grade. ModernTCN-delta achieved the lowest ten-seed mean grade MAE
among the four tested estimators (0.59888$^{\circ}$; registered P95 absolute
error, 1.67777$^{\circ}$). In 60 matched route--seed pairs, Fusion-LPV-MPC
reduced mean scheduled-grade MAE from 0.50260$^{\circ}$ to 0.41007$^{\circ}$
and reduced the mean of each primary closed-loop metric relative to
MTCN-LPV-MPC. Hierarchical paired intervals supported reductions in
heading-error RMS and the input-increment index, whereas the favorable
lateral-error effect remained statistically unresolved. Seventeen of 18
route--metric means favored or matched Fusion, indicating a predominantly
favorable but route-dependent benefit. ModernTCN-delta achieved a same-backend
core-inference p95 of 0.4038~ms. These results support history-only temporal grade
estimation with bounded adaptive correction for slope-conditioned LPV-MPC
within the frozen simulation benchmark.
```

```latex
\section{Introduction}

Automated guided vehicles (AGVs) are widely used in smart manufacturing,
automated warehousing, and industrial logistics, where they undertake material
handling and flexible transport tasks
\citep{Zhang2023AGVSurvey,Oyekanlu2020AGVReview,Bhargava2024AGVReview}.
Reliable AGV operation depends on perception, localization, path planning,
scheduling, and motion control. Path tracking is the final execution layer
that determines whether a planned route can be followed accurately and
smoothly under actuator constraints and changing operating conditions. This
study focuses on path tracking of a diagonal dual-drive-and-steer AGV along
industrial routes with longitudinal grade changes.

Linear parameter-varying model predictive control (LPV-MPC) is attractive for
vehicle and AGV path tracking because it handles multivariable dynamics,
reference preview, and actuator constraints within a unified optimization
framework
\citep{Manikandan2022CurveAwareMPCAGV,Alcala2020AutonomousRacingLPVMPC,Kedir2024PSOLPVMPC}.
For the considered AGV, vehicle speed, yaw rate, and longitudinal road grade
form the scheduling vector. The same grade also enters the measured-disturbance
channel, slope-feedforward term, scheduled weights, and scheduled input
envelopes. Omitting or misestimating grade can therefore make the prediction
model and optimization problem inconsistent with the plant, increasing
tracking and command-regularity errors. Direct inertial grade estimates may be
biased by transient acceleration, pitch dynamics, low-cost sensor noise, and
road-induced vibration \citep{Liu2023RoadSlopeAAIMM,Gao2024RoadSlopeHDV}.
Model-based observers can infer grade from vehicle dynamics, wheel-speed, and
driving-force information, but their accuracy remains sensitive to parameter
mismatch, load variation, maneuvers, and wheel--ground assumptions
\citep{Gao2024RoadSlopeHDV}. These limitations motivate a temporal estimator
that learns grade-related patterns from coupled proprioceptive histories while
retaining a physically interpretable secondary source.

One-dimensional convolutional networks are effective at extracting local
patterns from engineering time series \citep{Bueno2023Inception1DCNN}, but an
online control estimator additionally requires an input contract that ends at
the current sample. Architectures or preprocessing pipelines that use
offline-centered windows would violate this requirement. Temporal convolutional
networks address long-range history through causal and dilated convolutions and
residual connections, and have been applied to industrial sequence modeling
\citep{Bai2018TCN,Cao2021TCNRSA}. The relevant requirement here is therefore a
history-only online mapping, rather than a general rejection of non-causal
convolutional architectures.

Standard TCN configurations are nevertheless not tailored to multivariate AGV
grade perception. Fixed dilation patterns may underrepresent short
grade-sensitive transients \citep{Cao2021TCNRSA}, while velocity, acceleration,
yaw response, wheel motion, and drive current must interact across channels
\citep{Jin2023MTGODE}. ModernTCN combines large-kernel depthwise temporal
filtering with pointwise channel mixing \citep{Luo2024ModernTCN}. These
principles provide the structural basis for the lightweight, fixed-window
estimator developed here; the implemented network is a task-specific variant,
not a claim of reproducing or universally improving the complete original
architecture.

History-based estimation alone does not resolve slope-aware path tracking. The
input representation must expose both persistent inclination and short
grade-sensitive changes, and the historical window must retain sufficient
vehicle-response context. The learned estimate and a force-balance observer
also have condition-dependent and correlated errors, so their combination
requires empirically calibrated uncertainty, online innovation consistency,
and a gain that remains adaptive below its nominal cap. Finally, the resulting
grade must enter every slope-dependent LPV-MPC update through one consistent
interface. These coupled representation, fusion, and scheduling requirements
define the perception-to-control problem addressed in this work.

The methodological contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A lightweight ModernTCN-inspired estimator infers longitudinal road grade
from a history-only window of onboard AGV responses using large-kernel temporal
filtering and channel mixing. The operating-condition and steering-direction
heads remain auxiliary to grade regression: both support representation
learning, only main-condition confidence calibrates fusion uncertainty, and the
steering-direction output is retained for diagnostics. Neither predicted label
directly schedules or switches the controller.
\item A causal 22-dimensional dynamics-informed proprioceptive contract is
augmented with multi-scale temporal differences at lags $\{1,2,4\}$, yielding
the 88-dimensional ModernTCN-delta input. The 128-sample history is treated as a
frozen implementation parameter motivated by the representative vehicle
response time rather than as an independent contribution.
\item A qualified causal force-balance observer is introduced as a
complementary source. An uncertainty-adaptive ModernTCN--observer interface
uses robust empirical total-error moments, cross-source error dependence, and
online innovation consistency to apply a non-degenerate, bounded, and
rate-limited correction to the primary ModernTCN estimate. Correction sign
changes traverse zero continuously, while invalid or rejected states fall back
exactly to ModernTCN.
\item The same conditioned grade is introduced consistently into the LPV
prediction model, measured-disturbance channel, slope-feedforward term,
scheduled weights, and scheduled input envelopes. Controller structure,
nominal settings, scheduling maps, admissible ranges, and update laws remain
frozen, so the comparison changes the scheduling source without case-specific
controller retuning.
\end{enumerate}

The evaluation first establishes the control relevance of grade scheduling and
then separates offline estimator selection, closed-loop transfer, observer
qualification, adaptive-gain behavior, matched Fusion effects, and estimator
core inference cost. Under the frozen same-domain protocol, ModernTCN-delta
provides the strongest offline grade estimate among the four tested models,
whereas the closed-loop evidence remains metric-dependent. The calibrated
Fusion comparison supports lower heading-error RMS and input increments, with
a favorable but statistically unresolved lateral-error effect. This structure
keeps performance inference distinct from qualification and protection gates
and avoids treating offline perception accuracy as a substitute for closed-loop
evaluation.
```
